Bass diffusion

F′=(p+qF)(1−F)

How does a new product spread through a market? Some people buy because of advertising, and others because someone they know already owns one. The equation is separable, and its solution through F(0) = F₀ is

F(t)=1−p+qR0e(p+q)t+q,R0=p+qF01−F0

Change how strongly advertising and word of mouth act, and see the adoption curve, the yearly sales, and the phase line respond together.

Where it comes from

Let F(t) be the fraction of a market's eventual buyers who have bought by year t. The remaining 1 − F have not bought yet. Each year a fraction of them buy:

  • p of them on their own, from advertising and media, whatever anyone else does;
  • qF of them through word of mouth, which grows with the share of people who already own one.

Multiplying the rate (p + qF) by the pool 1 − F gives the equation above. Frank Bass proposed it in 1969 to forecast sales of new consumer durables.

What the terms mean

  • p, coefficient of innovation: the rate at which people adopt independently. It sets how fast sales start.
  • q, coefficient of imitation: how strongly adopters persuade the people who have not bought. It sets how sharp the S-curve is.
  • F′: sales per year as a share of the market. Multiply by the market size m to get units.
  • A meta-analysis of published estimates (Sultan, Farley & Lehmann, 1990) found average values near p ≈ 0.03 and q ≈ 0.38 per year: imitation usually matters far more than innovation.

Solving it (Section 1.4)

Separate the variables and split the left side with partial fractions:

1(p+qF)(1−F)=1p+q(qp+qF+11−F)

Integrating both sides gives

1p+qlnp+qF1−F=t+C

So R = (p + qF)/(1 − F) grows exactly exponentially, like e(p+q)t. Solving for F gives the formula above. The constant solution F = 1, lost when dividing, is the saturated market.

Reading the phase line (Section 2.2)

  • Equilibria: F = 1 (everyone has bought, stable) and F = −p/q (unstable, and outside the market whenever p > 0).
  • Peak sales: F′ is a downward parabola in F, highest at F* = (q − p)/(2q). Since F″ = (dF′/dF)·F′, the sales peak is also the inflection point of the S-curve. From F₀ = 0 it happens at t* = ln(q/p)/(p + q).
  • Special cases: q = 0 is the linear equation F′ = p(1 − F) of Section 1.5, with no S-shape. p = 0 is the logistic equation of Section 2.1. Then F = 0 is also an equilibrium, so word of mouth alone cannot start a market.
  • Left out: price, competing products, repeat purchases, and a market size that changes over time.

Predict, then check

Start from the typical durable and double q to 0.76. Does peak sales come earlier or later? Higher or lower?

Earlier and higher. The peak moves from t* = ln(0.38/0.03)/0.41 ≈ 6.2 years to ln(0.76/0.03)/0.79 ≈ 4.1 years. The peak rate (p + q)²/(4q) rises from about 11% to about 21% of the market per year. A faster spread means a shorter, sharper sales cycle.

Set p = 0 and F₀ = 0. What happens, and why does the phase line explain it?

Nothing happens. With p = 0, F′ = qF(1 − F) is the logistic equation, and F = 0 becomes an equilibrium. Word of mouth needs someone to talk. Raise F₀ a little and the S-curve comes back, but it starts more slowly the smaller F₀ is. This is one reason firms give out free samples and launch to early users.

Make p larger than q. Where does the sales curve peak now?

At launch. F* = (q − p)/(2q) is negative, so on 0 ≤ F ≤ 1 the parabola only decreases, and sales start high and fall from the beginning. The adoption curve is concave, with no S-shape and no inflection point. Heavily advertised products with little word of mouth look like this.

In the typical durable, innovation happens at only 3% a year. Roughly what share of all buyers are innovators?

About 21%. Adding up p(1 − F) over the whole diffusion gives (p/q) ln((p + q)/p) ≈ (0.03/0.38) ln(13.7). Early on, before many people own the product, almost every sale comes from innovation. This is the teal area in plot 2.

How are the curves computed?

Every curve uses the exact solution above. It is written as F = 1 − (p + q)/(R + q) so it stays finite when R = R₀e(p+q)t becomes very large. No numerical ODE solver is used. The peak year, the time to reach half the market, and the innovators' share are also exact. The share is (p/q) ln((p + q)/(p + qF₀))/(1 − F₀), from integrating p/(p + qF) over F.

The slope field draws equal-length segments at their true on-screen angle, shaded from shallow to steep. In plot 2 the two shaded areas add up to F′(t), and their total area is the share of the market that adopts.
