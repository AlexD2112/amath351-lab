A curve becomes a sequence of local decisions

The dashed segment previews one Euler step. Its slope is f(xₙ, yₙ), evaluated at the current approximate point. The horizontal change is h and the vertical change is h f(xₙ, yₙ). After taking that step, we update the slope.

The polygon is an approximation. Its segments need not be tangent to the exact solution through the original initial point: later Euler points usually lie off that curve.

Try this in class
  1. Keep h = 1. Predict the first two slopes and take two steps.
  2. Go to the endpoint, then reveal the exact solution. How large is the error?
  3. Turn on h/2, then try h = 0.1. Compare all approximations at the same endpoint.
  4. Switch to the HW 2 equation and repeat with h = 0.2 and h = 0.1.

For these smooth examples, reducing h reduces the endpoint error. Euler’s global error is O(h) on a fixed interval under the usual regularity assumptions; this is not a guarantee for every equation or every step size.
