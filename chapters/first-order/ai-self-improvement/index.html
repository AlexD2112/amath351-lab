Section 2.1 · Growth models

AI Self-Improvement Simulator

As AI becomes more capable, does each unit of research capacity become better or worse at producing further improvement?

A′ = A k(A)

A is a normalized index of effective research capacity. These are hypothetical models of a feedback process, not forecasts of real AI progress.

Change the assumption. Watch the consequences.

First read k(A) and the phase line. Predict whether capacity will decline, approach a limit, or keep growing.

1. The rule: relative growth k(A)

Positive → growth · negative → decline

2. The result: capacity A(t)

The axes remain fixed during playback. Curves leaving the plot continue in the numeric readouts. For A > 0, the sign of k(A) gives the direction of change; A = 0 is an equilibrium even when k(0) ≠ 0.

One feedback loop, three assumptions

ModelRelative rate k(A)Consequence for A₀ > 0
ExponentialrUnbounded as t → ∞; constant doubling time.
Logisticr(1 − A/K)Approach the stable equilibrium K.
Extinction–explosionαA − δDecay below A꜀, equilibrium at A꜀, finite-time blow-up above it.

The threshold is A꜀ = δ/α. Here “extinction” means effective research activity fading as capacity is lost. It does not mean an AI's learned knowledge is erased. K is a hypothetical limit under fixed conditions.

Exact solutions and the end of a trajectory

For exponential growth, A(t) = A₀ exp(rt). For logistic growth, A(t) = K / [1 + (K/A₀ − 1) exp(−rt)].

For the threshold model with δ > 0,

A(t) = A꜀ A₀ / [A₀ + (A꜀ − A₀) exp(δt)].

If A₀ > A꜀, the denominator first vanishes at T* = ln[A₀/(A₀ − A꜀)] / δ. The solution is defined only for t < T*. The displayed branch is never continued through its singularity.

With δ = 0, A′ = αA² and A(t) = A₀/(1 − αA₀t), ending at T* = 1/(αA₀). All curves are evaluated from these formulas; no numerical ODE integration is used.

What is held equal in “Compare the three”?

Each model starts at the same A₀ and has the same initial relative rate q, so A′(0) = qA₀ for all three. We use exponential r = q; logistic K = 5A₀ and r = 5q/4; and threshold α = 2q/A₀ with δ = q. The threshold is A₀/2.

The starting value and tangent agree. Later, the relative rates diverge. In comparison mode, time continues after the threshold trajectory ends so you can follow the other two solutions.

Can early observations reveal the long-term mechanism?

Suppose we know only A(0) = 1 and A(3) = 8. Both A(t) = 2ᵗ and the logistic curve A(t) = 10 / [1 + 9 exp(−rt)], with r = ln(36)/3, match those observations. At t = 6, they predict 64 and approximately 9.93 respectively.

Matching a small number of observations does not identify a unique growth law.

Do “strong RSI” and “diminishing returns” determine an equation?

An increasing relative rate does not by itself guarantee finite-time blow-up. For A′ = A ln A with A₀ > 1, k′(A) = 1/A > 0, but A(t) = A₀exp(t) stays finite at every finite time.

Diminishing returns do not necessarily imply a ceiling either. For A′ = rA/(1 + A/K), relative growth falls, while absolute growth approaches rK. The solution remains unbounded and becomes approximately linear.

For a smooth positive growth law f(A) along the entire path A ≥ A₀, the time to unbounded growth is the improper integral ∫A₀∞ dA/f(A). Whether this integral is finite depends on how fast f grows.

Actual AI development also depends on compute, data, human research, and the choice of capability measure. These experiments explore the consequences of assumptions.
